Developing Denver

Denver's Historic Pool Is Finally Getting Its $14.5 Million Rebuild - LINK
The long shuttered Mestizo-Curtis Park Pool in Five Points is getting a $14.5 million rebuild with a 50 meter lap pool, a separate leisure pool, and a diving board, with construction targeted for 2027 and reopening in 2028.

Kaia Residences Just Topped Out at 18 Stories in Capitol Hill - LINK
Trailbreak Partners' $160 million, 295 unit apartment tower at 8th and Lincoln reached full height in October and just added cold plunge tubs and a wellness marketplace to its amenity list ahead of a 2027 opening.

Crews Just Broke Ground on the First Phase of Cherry Creek West - LINK
East West Partners and Ascentris kicked off excavation on the 13 acre Cherry Creek West site this month, the first step toward roughly 400 homes, 200,000 square feet of office space, and a new underground garage.

Denver Pavilions Finally Has a Real Plan for What Comes Next - LINK
A new Urban Land Institute report recommends turning part of the struggling downtown mall into free public green space linking Union Station, Skyline Park, and Civic Center, while keeping the movie theater building intact for now.

Colorado Just Approved 14 Million Dollars to Keep Five Growing Companies In State - LINK
The Colorado Economic Development Commission approved up to $14.5 million in job growth tax credits for five companies weighing expansion here, led by a $6.4 million deal tied to 243 new jobs in Adams County.

Denver News

Denver Police Had to Remind Everyone Grand Theft Auto Is Still a Felony - LINK
The second GTA 6 dropped, Denver and Aurora police both posted lighthearted warnings that stealing cars is still a crime outside the video game, complete with an AI generated police illustration done in the game's own art style.

A Denver Builder Is Suing the City Over Its Affordable Housing Fees - LINK
RedT Homes argues Denver's mandatory affordable housing ordinance illegally charges it $12,000 per door on a small Rosedale townhome project, and the case just got argued in front of the 10th Circuit Court of Appeals.

Denver Is Weighing $822,000 in Raises for the Mayor's Own Appointees - LINK
A new city council proposal would hike pay for 12 of Mayor Mike Johnston's top appointees by 10 to 60 percent starting January 1, plus lock in automatic raises every four years without another council vote.

Broncos Tackle Garett Bolles Just Gave 5 Million Dollars to His Junior College - LINK
Bolles and his wife are funding a new academic center at Snow College in Utah, the small junior college that gave him his start, and he has not allowed a sack in 1,231 consecutive snaps since 2024.

Denver Just Reported Its First Mpox Case of the Season - LINK
State health officials confirmed one metro area case involving the more serious clade I strain and are urging at risk residents to get the Jynneos vaccine, even as they call the overall public risk low.

A New Audit Found Seven Straight Years of Deficits at the National Western Center - LINK
More than a decade after voters approved $778 million for the project, Denver's city auditor says there is still no clear path to financial sustainability and almost no accountability for measurable goals.

Denver Democrats Are Demanding a School Board Reverse a Controversial Censure - LINK
The party's executive committee voted to back school board VP Monica Hunter after an outside investigator called antisemitism allegations against her an inconclusive "she said, she said, he said," setting up a fight with the board president.

Denver Is About to Close Its First Hotel Turned Homeless Shelter - LINK
The city is shutting down one of its hotel based shelters as part of a strategy shift toward permanent housing, a move landing the same week advocates are already pushing back on other city homelessness decisions.

Denver Just Diverted Funding Away from a Bike and Pedestrian Project - LINK
The city redirected money that had been earmarked for walking and biking infrastructure this month, a move transportation advocates are watching closely given Denver's long running track record on bike funding.

Denver's Homeless Union Staged a Protest with the Coalition for the Homeless - LINK
Members and organizers rallied this week over labor conditions at one of the city's largest homelessness service providers, adding another flashpoint to a rough week for Denver's homelessness response system.

Wayfair Just Opened Its First Denver Area Store at Northfield - LINK
The furniture retailer opened its first Denver area location at Northfield this month, giving local shoppers a new option right as holiday furniture shopping season kicks in.

Denver Volunteer Opportunity

Food Bank of the Rockies, Warehouse Volunteer - LINK
With Thanksgiving week driving up demand across Denver's hunger relief network, Food Bank of the Rockies needs volunteers at its Denver distribution center to sort, repack, and prepare food headed out to hunger relief programs statewide. Shifts run Monday through Saturday, split between a morning block from 8:45 AM to noon and an afternoon block from 1 to 4 PM, no experience required, though volunteers must be at least 10 years old and able to lift up to 30 pounds. Sign up through the organization's VolunteerHub portal to claim a shift.

Hi 👋 I’m Ryan

I started Real Good Denver because I wanted one place that made Denver feel more connected. A publication focused on the people, businesses, events, restaurants, neighborhoods, and stories that give the city its energy.

I’ve spent the last decade building media companies, hosting podcasts, interviewing founders, and helping brands grow through storytelling and community.

I was born and raised in Denver (Asbury, Whittier, Baker, South, Metro, CU - Sko Buffs) and have bounced around the neighborhoods (DU, Observatory, S Broadway, Sunnyside, Sloans Lake) with my wife and two kids.
