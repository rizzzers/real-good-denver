New Denver Restaurants

Soda Club - LINK
Soft-opened the week of August 24, 1260 25th Street, RiNo (ground floor of the Ramble Hotel). Official grand opening set for September 1.
This Michelin Bib Gourmand-recognized import from New York brings plant-based Italian cooking to Denver, with housemade pastas, Roman-style pizzas, and an extensive natural wine list, in a space nearly double the size of the original NYC restaurant.

Hearth Patisserie - LINK

Developing Denver

Denver's Data Center Giant Chases a $30 Billion Valuation - LINK
Cherry Creek based data center firm Crusoe is reportedly in talks to raise $3 billion at a $30 billion valuation, a massive leap that cements Denver's growing reputation as ground zero for the AI infrastructure boom.

Colorado Rocket Maker Blasts Toward Wall Street - LINK
Berthoud based rocket and hypersonics company Ursa Major is going public through a $2.3 billion SPAC deal, fresh capital that will accelerate production of its rocket engines and missile systems and keep high tech aerospace jobs growing along the Front Range.

Denver's Nature and Science Museum Gets a Bright New Front Door - LINK
The Denver Museum of Nature & Science reopens its $20 million Sturm Family Wing on September 1, complete with a redesigned plaza, an upgraded theater and better accessibility, giving one of the city's most beloved institutions a fresh start for the next generation.

Santa Fe Drive Gets a $29 Million Glow Up - LINK
Construction has officially begun on a $29 million makeover of Santa Fe Drive, bringing wider sidewalks, new lighting, public art and fresh landscaping to the Art District between 6th and 13th avenues, a long term investment in one of Denver's most walkable strips.

217 New Homes Take Shape Near Northfield - LINK
Updated plans for 217 new rental townhomes near the Shops at Northfield are moving through Denver's design review, adding much needed housing, including 22 income restricted units, plus parks and pedestrian paths to a fast growing corner of northeast Denver.

Denver News

You Live in Denver? Actually, You Live in a Medieval Barony - LINK
Members of the Society for Creative Anachronism's Barony of Caerthe sword fight, forge armor, and hold feasts across the metro area, all under a medieval kingdom that technically includes Colorado, New Mexico, and Wyoming. One baroness works an office job by day and rules a fiefdom by night.

Denver Schools Just Went Silent, On Purpose - LINK
DPS enacted a bell to bell phone ban covering lunch, passing periods, and even smartwatches, but parents are split. Some worry they cannot reach their kids during a lockdown, while a district leader who tried it years ago swears it made students actually talk to each other again.

Denver Neighbors Are Snitching on Each Other's Sprinklers - LINK
With Denver's spring streamflow at its lowest point in 110 years, Denver Water fielded over 6,000 anonymous drought tip offs since May and handed out $7,250 in fines. Starting October 1, lawn watering gets banned outright, and one HOA has already sent 28 notices over brown grass.

A Colorado Mountain Coaster Ride Turned Into a Nightmare - LINK
A 60 year old rider was thrown from her seat belted cart on Copper Mountain's Rocky Mountain Coaster and dragged along the track, suffering a brain injury and collapsed lung. Her husband, riding in the cart right behind her, watched the entire thing happen.

Should Denver Rip Up Five Points' Own Train Tracks? - LINK
The L line has sat suspended on Welton Street for maintenance, and now the city is weighing whether to fix the tracks, redesign the corridor for pedestrians instead, or scrap transit there entirely. One longtime rider called scrapping it "astounding" after years of being pushed off the line.

Robotaxis Are Coming to Denver, Whether RTD Likes It or Not - LINK
Waymo is rolling out driverless taxis across a 50 mile radius of downtown within weeks, calling Denver its first true winter city test. Rideshare drivers worry about losing their jobs, and federal officials have already warned the cars can block ambulances mid emergency.

Cale Makar Just Became Hockey's First $20 Million Man - LINK
The Avalanche locked up their star defenseman through age 35 with an eight year, $163.2 million extension, the richest contract in NHL history. Makar just became the first defenseman in three decades to score 20 goals in three straight seasons.

Denver's Top Lawyer Faces a Second Ethics Complaint in Two Days - LINK
City Attorney Miko Brown is accused of steering Denver International Airport legal work toward her old law firm, already under fire for billing the city $800 an hour. The Board of Ethics meets next month to decide whether either complaint deserves a full investigation.

The Abandoned Tow Truck That Started a Colorado Cleanup Movement - LINK
A red tow truck sat rusting on a mountainside for 11 months after sliding off the road, becoming a viral symbol of Colorado's abandoned vehicle problem. Its rescue capped a saga that turned an online feud between its owner and a wilderness cleanup activist into an actual partnership.

Denver's $175 Million Street Project Got a Failing Grade - LINK
An audit of the 16th Street makeover found an $18 million cost overrun and a nearly year long delay that skipped City Council approval entirely. The city also quietly waived over $1 million in penalties against its own contractor for missing deadlines.

Denver's Historic Concert Hall Is Selling Naming Rights to Survive - LINK
The Colorado Symphony needs $15 million more for a $120 million overhaul of Boettcher Concert Hall, so it is auctioning off names for lobbies, seats, and the building around it. The concert hall's own name is protected, but everything else might not stay that way for long.

Denver settles into a warm, unsettled start to the week with highs in the mid to upper 80s and afternoon storm chances Monday and Tuesday, then the sky clears out and the heat cranks up, with sunny skies and highs climbing into the low 90s by midweek and holding there into the weekend.
