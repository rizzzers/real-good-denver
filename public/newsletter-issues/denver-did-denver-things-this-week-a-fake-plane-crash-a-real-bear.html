Denver News

Coach Prime Ducks a Process Server - LINK
A Boulder County deputy showed up to serve Deion Sanders court papers at an open CU practice, but Sanders was suddenly unavailable. The subpoena, tied to a bankruptcy case involving his son Shilo, eventually reached his lawyers in Denver instead.

DIA Staged a Fake Plane Crash With 400 Actors - LINK
Denver International Airport's federally mandated disaster drill featured a burning fuselage, gory prosthetic wounds, and hundreds of volunteers playing victims and grieving families so first responders could practice a real emergency.

A Mama Bear and Cub Are Roaming Southwest Denver - LINK
Colorado Parks and Wildlife has logged nearly 6,200 bear reports so far this year, almost double last year's pace, as drought pushes hungry bears out of the foothills and into Harvey Park backyards.

A Tranquilized Bear Took a Rough Tumble Out of a Platt Park Tree - LINK
Wildlife officers darted a 100 pound black bear hiding in a tree near Louisiana and Pennsylvania, and it dropped straight through the branches. Officials say the bear was fine and got relocated out of the city.

Denver Is Suing Big Tobacco Over Cigarette Butt Litter - LINK
City Hall is suing nine tobacco manufacturers, including Philip Morris and Altria, arguing cigarette filters were designed knowing they'd clog storm drains and litter city streets, with Mayor Mike Johnston pushing for biodegradable filters instead of taxpayers footing the cleanup bill.

Denver's $800-an-hour Lawyer Bill Is Putting the City's Budget Crunch on Blast - LINK
Denver is paying a private law firm as much as $800 an hour, including a two-years-out-of-law-school associate who billed $190,000 in four months, all while the city faces a $200 million shortfall and just laid off 169 workers.

Denver's $4.7 Million Gunshot Detector Might Get the Boot - LINK
Denver police are shopping for a replacement for their decade-old ShotSpotter system after it logged over 25,000 gunshot alerts since 2020 but led to just 655 arrests, reigniting a fight over whether the tech actually works or mostly just cries wolf.

 

Denver Events

Dayna Copeland Open Studio at Hudson Gardens - LINK
Tuesday, August 25, Hudson Gardens, Littleton, Free.
Watch collage artist Dayna Copeland build layered paper pieces live inside Hudson Gardens' working studio space. It's a quiet, hands-on peek at process over polish, tucked among the trellises south of Denver, with garden admission waived for the afternoon.

World Famous Waffle Wednesdays: International Dog Day - LINK
Wednesday, August 26, Battery 621, Free.
Denver's longtime free breakfast mixer throws open its patio to dogs for International Dog Day, waffles for humans, a chance to meet adoptable pups, and a crowd of regulars who've been showing up before work for years. Bring a leash and an appetite.

Evening Bird Walk at Belmar Park - LINK
Wednesday, August 26, Heritage Lakewood Belmar Park, Free.
Denver Audubon volunteers lead a slow, low key evening walk through Belmar Park's wetlands as birds settle in for the night. No binoculars required and no birding experience needed, just a couple hours outside with people who genuinely love herons.

La Raza Park Day and Cruise - LINK
Thursday, August 27, La Raza Park, Free.
Denver's Chicano community reclaims the Northside with lowriders cruising Federal Boulevard, music, food vendors, and family history on display at a park residents once fought to keep from being renamed. A genuinely local tradition, not a tourist stop.

16 LIVE: Mile High Hits - LINK
Thursday, August 27, Glenarm Plaza, Free.
Colorado DJs, dancers, and artists take over a downtown plaza for an evening built entirely around homegrown creative talent. Expect a mix of sound and movement you won't find on a typical festival bill, free and outdoors in the heart of downtown.

Mike D "Thank You" Listening Party - LINK
Friday, August 28, Twist and Shout Records, Free.
Beastie Boys member Mike D's solo debut gets an in-store listening party, complete with exclusive purple vinyl available only at this event and freebies for whoever shows up. No RSVP, just first come first served on East Colfax.

10th Annual Colorado Dashiki Festival - LINK
Saturday, August 29, Centennial Center Park Amphitheater, Free.
Colorado's biggest African cultural festival marks a decade with music, fashion, food, and thousands of attendees celebrating dashiki style and heritage just south of Denver. It's grown from a small community gathering into one of the metro's most colorful annual celebrations.

Denver Taiko's 50th Anniversary Concert: Keio Kairai - LINK
Saturday, August 29, Green Center Bunker Auditorium, Colorado School of Mines, Golden, Free.
Denver Taiko has been pounding drums in this city since before most of its members were born, and this concert marks half a century of the group. Titled Keio Kairai, honoring the past while shaping the future, it's a rare full stage show from a Colorado institution.

Colorado Dragon Boat Festival - LINK
Saturday and Sunday, August 29-30, Sloan's Lake Park, Free.
Teams race dragon boats across Sloan's Lake while the shoreline fills with Asian food vendors and cultural performances at one of Denver's largest heritage festivals. Races start at 8am both mornings, but the food and crowds make it worth a visit any time of day.

Rooster ArtFest - LINK
Saturday, August 29, ReelWorks Denver, Free.
More than 100 local artists, makers, and creative vendors fill RiNo's ReelWorks with work you won't find in a big box gallery, plus live performances including a headlining set from Kota the Friend. A scrappy, homegrown art fair with real staying power.

Heller High Water at Stanley Beer Hall - LINK

A free early evening set from local band Heller High Water fills the Stanley Beer Hall patio with sound while the marketplace hums around it. Grab a beer, catch some tunes, and treat it like a low key Sunday hangout rather than a show.

Weekly Denver Forecast

Denver stays properly summery for the week, with highs running 88 to 93 degrees and overnight lows settling into the low to mid 60s. Wednesday is the wettest bet with a 50 percent chance of afternoon thunderstorms, while Tuesday, Thursday, Friday and Saturday all carry a 40 percent shot at their own pop-up storms too. Keep an umbrella in the car: this is classic late-August Front Range monsoon pattern, hot and sunny mornings, then storms build fast after lunch.
