Developing Denver

Denver Goes Driverless - LINK
Waymo's self-driving cars started charging Denver riders on September 1 across a 60-square-mile zone, with the company promising to keep expanding vehicles and coverage, putting Denver in an elite club of cities where you can hail a robot instead of an Uber.

Amazon Bets Big on Aurora - LINK
Amazon dropped 23.6 million dollars on 70 acres in Aurora to build a 1.2 million square foot distribution center that could bring up to 1,200 new jobs to the metro, another sign the online giant is doubling down on Colorado's delivery network.

Ikon Pass Parent Picks Downtown Over Utah - LINK
Alterra Mountain Company, the outfit behind the Ikon Pass, turned down a move to Salt Lake City and will instead relocate its headquarters into the old Denver Post building, occupying two floors under a 12-year lease starting in 2027 and anchoring a new downtown outdoor recreation hub.

Golden Becomes Ground Zero for Geothermal - LINK
The US Department of Energy opened a new Geothermal Center of Excellence at the National Laboratory of the Rockies in Golden, pairing oil and gas drilling know how with public private partnerships to push underground heat power toward commercial scale.

Englewood Dreams Up a Transit Village - LINK
New concept plans for Englewood CityCenter near the light rail station could bring up to 1,177 homes, 53,000 square feet of retail, a signature hotel, and an expanded civic park, turning an aging mall site into a walkable mixed use village.

Denver News

Denver Walkout Turns Into a Shoving Match - LINK
Hundreds of students from more than 200 Colorado schools walked out on September 4, and the Denver rally at La Alma-Lincoln Park turned tense when counter-protesters showed up, leaving three people hospitalized and police investigating at least five separate altercations.

Denver Voters Are Souring on Their Mayor - LINK
A new Colorado Polling Institute survey shows Mayor Mike Johnston's favorability sliding from 48 percent in 2024 to just 39 percent this year, even as 57 percent of voters credit him with progress on homelessness, his signature issue.

A Reckless Pass Wipes Out a Community Garden - LINK
A driver trying to pass another car at Elmira Street and 11th Avenue in Aurora swerved into oncoming traffic and plowed through Isabel Mendoza's community garden, destroying roughly a fifth of the vegetables she grows for local nonprofits.

A Man With a Rubber Knife Rattles Mayor Johnston's Security - LINK
A man approached Mayor Mike Johnston at Union Station wielding what looked like a real knife, prompting the mayor's security officer to draw his gun, before the man bent the blade to prove it was plastic and calmly walked away before police arrived.

Is Garth Brooks Secretly Playing the Grizzly Rose? - LINK
Denver fans are convinced Garth Brooks will show up as mystery act Yukon Jack at the Grizzly Rose while he's in town for three Ball Arena shows, pointing to his 1993 use of the same alias and a hometown named Yukon, Oklahoma.

Denver Is About to Go Quiet on Gunshot Detection - LINK
Denver's ShotSpotter contract expires in December with no replacement system lined up, meaning the city will lean on 911 calls alone while officials spend the next year deciding whether the controversial gunshot detection technology, long criticized for its uneven deployment, should come back at all.

Colorado's November Ballot Is Loaded With Landmines - LINK
Colorado voters face more than a dozen November ballot measures, including a Democratic plan to tax incomes over 500,000 dollars for schools and healthcare, a measure requiring police to report undocumented immigrants charged with crimes, and dueling bans on transgender youth in sports and gender-affirming care.

Denver Ordered to Double-Check a Downtown Tower's Bones - LINK
This week's real estate lawsuit roundup includes an order for Denver to reinvestigate a downtown office tower's structural integrity, Longmont neighbors suing to block an equestrian center, and a Thornton homeowner suing Spruce Power over solar panels that allegedly stopped working entirely.

This Denver Hotel Just Lost Its Licenses, Again - LINK
The city suspended liquor, food, and cabaret licenses at Nativ Hotel and Gatsby Social for the second time this year after a gang-related shooting wounded a woman, a violent brawl forced a neighboring hotel into lockdown, and unlicensed security guards were found on staff.

RTD Finally Wins a Round Against Copper Thieves - LINK
RTD logged just eight copper wire thefts this year compared to 25 by this point in 2025, a 68 percent drop that police credit to more cameras, tighter cooperation with scrap yards, and a new app that lets riders flag suspicious activity in real time.

Denver's Building Boom Is Running Out of Workers - LINK
Colorado's construction industry is losing workers to data center projects and tougher immigration enforcement, with 46 percent of the state's contractors reporting cost-driven project cancellations, compared to 34 percent nationwide, as credential requirements tighten across job sites.

 

Denver Events

Star Trek 60th Anniversary Party - LINK
Tuesday, September 8, Fiction Beer Company, Free
Fiction Beer Company hosts local fan group Denver Ten Forward for a taproom takeover marking 60 years since Star Trek's original premiere, with classic episodes on the TVs and themed snacks. It is a free, low key way to spend a Tuesday with people who actually know what a tribble is.

Silent Film: The General (1926) - LINK
Wednesday, September 9, Chautauqua Auditorium, Boulder, $15
Chautauqua's long running silent film series brings back Buster Keaton's Civil War comedy with live piano accompaniment by Rodney Sauer, no dialogue and no phones required. It is one of the most purely charming ways to spend a Wednesday night in Boulder.

Boulder Burner Meet-up - LINK
Wednesday, September 9, Junkyard Social Club, Boulder, Free
A monthly, no ticket needed gathering for Boulder's Burning Man community at Junkyard Social Club, the closest thing Colorado has to a playa clubhouse. Drop in to swap stories, plan art cars, or just eavesdrop on what burners talk about in the off season.

Bass Bingo: ATLiens Edition - LINK
Thursday, September 10, Larimer Lounge, Free
Larimer Lounge's weekly bingo night pairs numbered balls with a DJ set, so cards fill up between bass drops instead of church basement silence. This ATLiens themed edition opens doors at 5 PM for the 21 and up crowd.

Hunger Games Trivia - LINK
Thursday, September 10, Fiction Beer Company, Free
Fiction Beer Company turns its taproom into District 12 for a trivia night pulled from the books and movies, complete with alliances and one eventual victor. Come with a team, or find one at the bar and hope they carry you through the reaping.

Smithsonian Starstruck: An Immersive Experience - LINK
Friday, September 11, York Street Yards, Ticketed
A shared VR pop up sends groups of up to six through real astronomical data, from Earth's observatories out past a black hole, using wireless headsets and digital avatars. It is equal parts science museum and video game, and not something Denver gets very often.

Cool Vibes Reggae Fest with The Wailers - LINK
Saturday, September 12, Levitt Pavilion Denver at Ruby Hill Park, Free
Levitt Pavilion's free summer series wraps up with a proper reggae festival headlined by The Wailers. Bring a blanket and skip the ticket line for one of the last big free outdoor shows before fall settles in.

Denver Zine Fest 2026 - LINK
Sunday, September 13, Denver Public Library: Central Library, Free
Denver Zine Library takes over the Central Library for a day of self published comics, poetry, and handmade zines, with tabling from local makers and workshops on the craft. It is the most low key, DIY afternoon on this list, and it costs nothing.

Silent Film: Kid Boots (1926) - LINK
Wednesday, September 16, Chautauqua Auditorium, Boulder, $15
Chautauqua's silent film series returns a week later with the Eddie Cantor comedy Kid Boots, accompanied live on piano by Hank Troy. These screenings draw a devoted, slightly older crowd that treats 100 year old slapstick with the same reverence other people save for opening night at a multiplex.

VAHNA Motorcycle Film Festival - LINK
Thursday, September 17, The Oriental Theater, Ticketed
The Oriental Theater hosts this touring festival of short motorcycle films, presented by Triumph, built for riders and non riders who just like a good road story. Expect documentary shorts, a packed house of leather jackets, and a few bikes parked out front for show.

WanderFest '26: 10th Anniversary Party - LINK
Thursday, September 17, New Terrain Brewing Co, Golden, Free
New Terrain Brewing turns ten with a three day, free entry anniversary bash featuring live music, food trucks, and rare barrel aged beer releases across four bars. Day one of the weekend lands right in this window, a solid excuse for a Thursday drive out west.
