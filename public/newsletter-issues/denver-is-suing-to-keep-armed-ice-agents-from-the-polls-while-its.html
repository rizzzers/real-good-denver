It’s a pretty great week to live in Denver if your preferred price for entertainment is zero dollars.

First, the Colorado Minerals, Fossils, Gems & Jewelry Show is taking over the Denver Coliseum through September 20. More than 300 vendors, half a million specimens, fossils, gems, minerals and actual dinosaurs. Admission is free. Parking is also free.

Meanwhile, Colorado Startup Week is happening through Friday with 250+ sessions, 200+ community events, founders, investors, builders, happy hours and enough people saying “agentic” to power downtown Denver for several days.

Also completely free.

So this week you can look at a 100-million-year-old fossil in the morning and hear about a startup founded three weeks ago in the afternoon.

Denver contains multitudes.

Go do stuff. Keep your money.

Alterra Boards the Old Denver Post Building - LINK
Colorado dangled 1.92 million dollars in tax credits and a 7 million dollar loan to keep the Ikon Pass parent from bolting to Utah, and it worked. Alterra is claiming two floors of the old Denver Post building, turning a symbolic vacancy into a 106 job outdoor hub by 2027.

An 18 Mile Bus Line Just Got a Green Light - LINK

Golden Becomes Ground Zero for Next Gen Geothermal - LINK

Governor Polis Takes Down a 510 Pound Sumo Wrestler - LINK
At a Japan America Society gala, Polis stepped into the ring for a ceremonial bout against Mongolian wrestler Ichinojo Takashi, who outweighed him by roughly 300 pounds, marking one of the stranger moments of the governor's tenure.

Denver School Board Members Accused of Slurs Demand Their Own Investigation - LINK
Vice President Monica Hunter and member DJ Torres are fighting accusations of antisemitic language and financial sloppiness by asking for an independent probe before the board can vote to censure them, turning routine DPS business into a very public airing of grievances.

A Century Old Colfax Dive Bar Gets a Last Minute Save - LINK
Nob Hill Inn was set to close for good after bus rapid transit construction throttled foot traffic on Colfax, but a community fundraiser and a wave of regulars showing up bought the century old dive a temporary reprieve while it searches for a longer term fix.

Denver Police Reopen 13 Cases Tied to a Disgraced Crime Lab Analyst - LINK
After retesting evidence handled by fired CBI analyst Missy Woods, who pleaded guilty to falsifying DNA results, Denver's crime lab found new leads eligible for the national DNA database, meaning old cases could finally produce new suspects years after Woods cut corners.

Denver Sues to Keep Armed ICE Agents Away From the Polls - LINK
The city and a coalition of Latino advocacy groups filed suit against the Trump administration, arguing that stationing armed immigration agents at voting sites would violate federal law and intimidate eligible voters just as Colorado heads into a contested midterm election season.

United Scraps a Fighter Jet Escorted 9/11 Flyover Over Denver - LINK
United had planned to mark the anniversary by flying a passenger jet over the metro with a fighter jet escort, but backed out after residents flooded the airline with complaints that a formation resembling an attack would be traumatic rather than commemorative.

Drought has pushed hungry bears deeper into Front Range neighborhoods and kitchens, and wildlife officers have already euthanized more than three times as many as last year, a grim preview of what a hotter, drier fall could mean for the next generation of Colorado bears.

Denver BAZAAR at Larimer Square - LINK
Saturday, September 19, Larimer Square, Free.
Colorado makers take over the city's oldest block for an evening market while the block's restaurants and patios stay open, so you can shop ceramics, vintage, and jewelry with a drink in hand. Dog friendly and all ages, it is a proper reason to linger downtown after dark.

Japan Fest with JA NE Cultural Mixtape - LINK
Sunday, September 20, Levitt Pavilion Denver, Free.

HQ Rock N' Roll Garage Sale - LINK
Sunday, September 20, HQ, Free.

GRUNCH: Bluegrass Grunge Brunch - LINK
Sunday, September 20, Ophelia's Electric Soapbox, Tickets required.
KBCO throws a brunch built around the most unlikely genre pairing in Denver: bluegrass pickers and grunge distortion sharing one stage over mimosas. It is 21 and over, mid afternoon, and exactly as strange and fun as it sounds.

Colorado Festival of Horror: Clowns vs. Witches - LINK

Not for the faint of heart, but very much for Denver's horror crowd.
